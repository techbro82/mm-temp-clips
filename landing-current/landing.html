Mortality

Marcus Aurelius

An emperor who ruled the world yet wrote each night only to master himself. On facing death, duty, and the ordering of a life.

Faith

Augustine of Hippo

A restless soul who walked the long road from appetite to peace, and left the map. On meaning and the hungers beneath it.

Adversity

Epictetus

Born a slave, he became the great teacher of endurance. On bearing what you cannot change, and mastering what you can.

Freedom

Frederick Douglass

He freed himself, then spent his life freeing others with words. On courage, dignity, and standing against wrong.

Craft

Benjamin Franklin

Printer, inventor, statesman, self-made in every sense. On discipline, industry, and the patient building of a life.

Leadership

Ulysses S. Grant

He led armies through the Civil War and wrote his memoirs while dying. On quiet resolve and finishing the hard task.

Wealth

Andrew Carnegie

He built an empire, then gave it all away. On earning well, and the duty to use what you gather for others.

Science

Charles Darwin

He changed the world not by brilliance but by patient looking. On observation, honesty, and following the evidence.

Thinking

Ralph Waldo Emerson

He argued the hardest thing is to trust your own mind. On self-reliance and thinking for yourself.

Nature

Henry David Thoreau

He went to the woods to live deliberately. On simplicity, attention, and what a life is truly made of.

Service

Jane Addams

She left comfort to live among the poor and build something lasting. On service that is humble, practical, and real.

Friendship

Cicero

Rome's greatest voice on what binds good people together. On loyalty, honour, and keeping faith across a life.
